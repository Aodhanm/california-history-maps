Sources & Method

Every map on this site is driven by an open dataset, and the great majority of the features in those datasets carry their own citation. This page gathers what the whole site is built on and says which map uses which. Across 14 published maps there are 1,331 features, 1,186 of them carrying at least one citation of their own, and 184 carrying a quoted passage from a source. This page is generated from the datasets, not maintained by hand, so it follows the data.

Two maps have their own fuller apparatus, because they were built to a higher standard of documentation than the rest and audited before publication: Lumber Ports and Lighthouses of California. Where those pages and this one differ in detail, they are the authority for their own map.

How to read a citation on these maps

A pin’s popup carries its own sources. Several conventions run across the site:

  • Coordinate precision is stated, not implied. A solid marker sits at a known place; a translucent or dashed marker is approximate within its district; a hollow marker is conjectural. Dashed route lines mean the party passed roughly this way.
  • Manuscript citations give both page systems. A document in the Savage transcripts is cited to the original folio or page and to Savage’s own pagination and the scan leaf, because the three do not agree.
  • Quoted matter is in quotation marks with a citation. Where a map paraphrases, it does not use the source’s wording. The site was audited for unquoted borrowing in September 2026 and the corrections applied.
  • A missing source is shown, not hidden. Where no source was found for something, the popup says so rather than leaving a confident blank.
  • Dates carry their grain. Some are exact to the day from a document; others are a year from the standard accounts, and say so.

The sources

Archival and manuscript sources

What the maps ultimately rest on. Where a feature has a manuscript behind it, the popup cites it.

  • Archives of California (BANC MSS C–A), The Bancroft Library, University of California, Berkeley — the Thomas Savage transcript copies, 63 volumes. The originals burned in 1906; Savage's copies are the record. Most features on this site that rest on a manuscript rest on these. Pins with a catalog record deep-link to the documentary calendar and from there to the manuscript leaf. Used by: Coastal Defense of San Francisco Bay, The Bodega–Ross Corridor, The Bay Area Imperial Frontier, Military Engagements in Spanish, Mexican & Conquest-Era California, Missions & Establishments, Gabriel Moraga's Expeditions, Native California, Population of the Californias, The Presidial System, The Secularization of the Missions
  • United States District Court land case files for California, The Bancroft Library (Northern and Southern District series, cited as ND / SD by case number). Digitised at digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu. The evidentiary basis for the rancho maps: each confirmed grant generated a case file with the claimant's title papers. Used by: Lumber Ports of the California Coast, Ranchos of Mexican California, Ranchos of Spanish & Mexican California, Ranchos, Pueblos & Presidios — Version B (NAHC base)
  • Online Archive of California and Calisphere — finding aids and digitised images from California repositories. Used by: Lumber Ports of the California Coast, Ranchos of Mexican California, Ranchos, Pueblos & Presidios — Version B (NAHC base)
  • Mariano Guadalupe Vallejo, Documentos para la Historia de California (BANC MSS C–B), The Bancroft Library — cited by volume and document. The companion collection to C–A, and the source for much of the presidio artillery and garrison detail. Used by: Coastal Defense of San Francisco Bay, Military Engagements in Spanish, Mexican & Conquest-Era California
  • Land Case Maps (diseños), The Bancroft Library — the hand-drawn grant sketches filed as evidence in the land cases. 1,397 catalogued for this project; the gallery reproduces them. Used by: Ranchos of Mexican California, Ranchos, Pueblos & Presidios — Version B (NAHC base)
  • Local historical societies and house museums — Kelley House Museum (Mendocino), Fort Ross Conservancy, Sonoma County Library heritage collections, Mill Valley Historical Society, Marin History Museum, Del Norte, Aptos, Cambria, Tomales and Salt Point local collections. Used by: Coastal Defense of San Francisco Bay, Lumber Ports of the California Coast
  • Presidio garrison and artillery register — an unpublished working register compiled for this project from the Archives of California transcripts and the printed accounts. Not yet published. Where a feature rests on it alone, the underlying documents are cited in the register and can be supplied on request. Used by: The Bodega–Ross Corridor, The Presidial System
  • San Francisco Maritime National Historical Park, J. Porter Shaw Library — Carl Christensen and Carlos A. Call photograph collections; Tooker and Lewis research files; Canright, “Coastwise Lumber Transportation.” Used by: Lumber Ports of the California Coast
  • Cal Poly Humboldt Special Collections — A. W. Ericson, Palmquist, Swanlund–Baker, Boyle and Schoenrock photograph collections. Used by: Lumber Ports of the California Coast

Printed primary sources

Published contemporaneously with the events, or printing documents whose originals are gone.

  • Bancroft, Hubert Howe. History of California, 7 vols. San Francisco: The History Company, 1884–1890 — including the Pioneer Register. Used as a printed primary compilation as much as a secondary work: Bancroft quotes and calendars documents whose originals are gone. Used by: Coastal Defense of San Francisco Bay, The Bodega–Ross Corridor, The Bay Area Imperial Frontier, Lumber Ports of the California Coast, Military Engagements in Spanish, Mexican & Conquest-Era California, Missions & Establishments, Gabriel Moraga's Expeditions
  • Gibson, James R., and Alexei A. Istomin, eds. Russian California, 1806–1860: A History in Documents — cited by document number. Cited in the data as “Gibson & Istomin, Doc n”. Used by: The Bodega–Ross Corridor, The Bay Area Imperial Frontier, Missions & Establishments
  • Russian-American Company material — documents from the Archive of the Foreign Policy of the Russian Empire (AVPRI), and Golovnin and Khlebnikov as cited in the data in short form, often at second hand through Farris and through Gibson & Istomin. Where a Russian document is reached through a translation or a secondary work rather than the archive, the citation says so. Used by: The Bodega–Ross Corridor, The Bay Area Imperial Frontier, Missions & Establishments
  • Period newspapers — Daily Alta California, Mendocino Beacon, Fort Bragg Advocate, Ukiah Republican Press, the San Francisco Call and Chronicle, the California Star. Via the California Digital Newspaper Collection and as quoted in the doghole-ports MPDF. Used by: Lighthouses of California, Lumber Ports of the California Coast, Military Engagements in Spanish, Mexican & Conquest-Era California
  • Davidson, George. Pacific Coast. Coast Pilot of California, Oregon, and Washington. Washington: GPO, 1869; 4th ed., 1889. The single most useful printed source for nineteenth-century coastal positions, landings and station descriptions. Used by: Lighthouses of California, Lumber Ports of the California Coast
  • U.S. Coast & Geodetic Survey topographic sheets (T-sheets) and harbour charts, 1852–1930s. NOAA Historical Map & Chart Collection. Used by: Lighthouses of California, Lumber Ports of the California Coast
  • Diary of Fr. Pedro Muñoz (Moraga expedition, 1806) and Diary of Fr. José María de Zalvidea (1806), as edited and translated in Cook. Used by: Military Engagements in Spanish, Mexican & Conquest-Era California, The 1806 Interior Expeditions — Zalvidea & Moraga
  • Wagner, Henry R. — the 1931 edition of the Spanish northwest-coast voyage accounts, cited in the data as “Wagner 1931” with page numbers. Used by: The Bodega–Ross Corridor, The Bay Area Imperial Frontier
  • Hoffman, Ogden. Reports of Land Cases Determined in the United States District Court for the Northern District of California. San Francisco, 1862. Used by: Lumber Ports of the California Coast, Ranchos of Spanish & Mexican California

  • Hittell, John S. The Commerce and Industries of the Pacific Coast. San Francisco: A. L. Bancroft, 1882. Used by: Lumber Ports of the California Coast
  • Jepson, Willis L. California Tanbark Oak. USDA Forest Service Bulletin 75. Washington: GPO, 1911. Used by: Lumber Ports of the California Coast
  • Annual List of Merchant Vessels of the United States, 1877–1924 (vessel tonnages), with Lyman's compilation from the registers. Used by: Lumber Ports of the California Coast
  • Historic American Engineering Record / Historic American Buildings Survey, Library of Congress — HAER No. CA-61 (schooner C. A. Thayer, 1988), No. CA-67 (steam schooner Wapama, 2001), and light-station documentation. Used by: Lumber Ports of the California Coast
  • Spanish hydrographic charts — the Plano del Puerto series and the Cartas esféricas, including the 1793 re-survey, from the Library of Congress and reproduced in this site’s gallery. Used by: The Bodega–Ross Corridor

Datasets, boundaries and geographic data

Where the lines and the coordinates come from. These carry their own uncertainties, stated below.

  • NAHC / California Natural Resources Agency, California Land Grants — grant boundaries reconstructed against the Public Land Survey System. One of two independent boundary sets on the site; the rancho maps exist in two versions precisely because the two disagree. Used by: Ranchos of Mexican California, Ranchos, Pueblos & Presidios — Version B (NAHC base)
  • ECAI / UCSD, Spanish & Mexican Land Grants of California (via the California Land Grants ArcGIS project) — boundary and land-case data. The base for the published rancho map. Used by: Ranchos of Spanish & Mexican California, Ranchos, Pueblos & Presidios — Version B (NAHC base)
  • USGS Geographic Names Information System (GNIS) — used to fix coordinates for named coastal features, cited by feature ID. Used by: Lighthouses of California, Lumber Ports of the California Coast
  • USGS National Hydrography Dataset and The National Map — stream flowlines, used to place inland railheads and creek-mouth landings. Used by: Lighthouses of California, Lumber Ports of the California Coast
  • Handbook of North American Indians, vol. 8: California (Smithsonian Institution, 1978) — tribal territory boundaries, as digitised by the Salton Sea Database Program, University of Redlands. Territory lines on a map imply a hard edge that the ethnography does not support. They are drawn as generalisations, and subgroup labels are positioned without boundaries implied. Used by: Native California
  • OpenStreetMap — surviving rail alignments, traced for the lumber railroads where a historic grade is still on the ground. Used by: Lumber Ports of the California Coast
  • David Rumsey Map Collection — georeferenced historical maps. Used by: Ranchos of Spanish & Mexican California

Basemaps

The maps are drawn with Leaflet over Esri’s World Topographic and Ocean basemaps (© Esri, HERE, Garmin, USGS, NGA, EPA, USDA, NPS, GEBCO, NOAA) and OpenStreetMap contributors. The basemap is a modern one in every case: no historical basemap underlies these pins, and modern coastlines, jetties and channels are not the ones the historical actors saw. Where that matters — a harbour entrance that has moved, a bar that has been dredged — the popup says so.

Companion projects

These maps are one of four related projects built on the same archive. Each is separately citable, and each is a source the others draw on.

  • Archives of California: A Documentary Calendar of the Savage Transcripts — the item-level catalogue of BANC MSS C–A 1–63. The manuscript layer under most of what is on these maps; pins with a catalog record deep-link into it. DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21912644
  • Ranchos of Alta California — 672 land grants and claims with their adjudication, the families that held them, and the manuscript diseños filed as evidence. Its Sources and Method page carries the land-case apparatus in full. The rancho maps here use a different boundary dataset from the portal, which is why the two disagree; both are published rather than reconciled.
  • California Ship Registry — every recorded vessel visit to the California coast, 2,072 visits carrying 2,900 citations, from the documentary calendar, Bancroft's narrative and marine lists, and Ogden's otter-trade appendix. The maritime counterpart to the landward record on these maps, and the eighteenth- and early nineteenth-century precursor to the coastwise traffic on the lumber-ports and lighthouse maps.
  • The diseño gallery — 1,397 manuscript grant maps from the Bancroft land-case files, reproduced.

What this page does not claim

  • It is a list of what the site draws on, not a bibliography of the field. Works read and not used are not here.
  • Grouped entries stand for families of citation. The exact citation for any single feature is in that feature’s popup and in the dataset, which is the authority.
  • Full bibliographic form is given where it is established in this site’s own audited apparatus or complete in the data. Where the data cites a work in short form, the short form is what appears here rather than an expansion that was never checked.
  • Coverage is uneven by design and by accident. Some maps rest on a long documented record; others rest on a thin one, and the thin ones say so on the map.

Corrections are welcome and are logged on the errata page.
